Same $2,000/month payment. Same 30-year mortgage. The gap is entirely a product of creditworthiness tier and the rate the lender charges — not effort or risk in any productive sense.
Credit spread function. The spread above the base rate is modelled as a continuous, monotonically decreasing function of population percentile, calibrated to observed anchors: bottom of population ~22pp spread (sub-prime / high-risk lending); 50th percentile ~9.5pp; 80th percentile ~4.5pp; 96th percentile ~2.4pp; 99th percentile ~1.5pp; top 0.01% ~0.5pp. This is a simplification — in reality, large fractions of the population cannot qualify for mortgage lending at all at high base rates. Loan formula: P = 2,000 × [1−(1+r)^(−360)] / r where r = (base + spread) / 12 / 100. At r=0, P = $720,000. The super-elite (top 0.01%) occupy 0.01% of the chart width — a single pixel or less — shown here as a callout at the right edge.
