Who qualifies — and who doesn't

The loan is fixed at $15,000. What changes with interest rates is the monthly payment required to service it — and whether your income, given the rate you'll actually be charged, is enough to qualify. People with lower incomes face higher credit risk premiums on top of the base rate, creating a compounding barrier. The slider shows how the eligibility boundary shifts across the population as base rates change.

Model. Loan: $15,000, 60-month term. Monthly payment = P × r × (1+r)⁶⁰ / ((1+r)⁶⁰ − 1) where r = (base + credit_spread) / 12 / 100. Eligibility threshold: monthly payment ≤ 15% of gross monthly income (a standard car-payment-to-income guideline used by Canadian and US lenders). Credit spreads above base rate are modelled as a continuous, monotonically decreasing function of population percentile, calibrated to observed Canadian auto-lending markets: bottom 10% face spreads of ~19–25pp (subprime/deep-subprime auto); median borrower ~9.5pp; top 10% ~3pp; top 1% ~2pp. Income distribution: approximate individual gross income distribution for Canada (2020, Statistics Canada). Person icons: each represents 1% of the working population. Eligible (green) = income sufficient to qualify at their effective rate. Ineligible (grey) = income below the threshold. Eligibility boundary shifts as base rate changes; credit spread structure does not change (it is set by lenders based on credit risk, independent of the base rate).
